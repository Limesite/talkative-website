A new trade fair is the only moment when every supplier relationship on the floor is up for grabs at once.

DSEI Germany runs for the first time from 9 to 12 March 2027 at the Hannover Exhibition Grounds, run by Clarion Events in partnership with Deutsche Messe. It is the German edition of the DSEI series, and it arrives at an unusual moment in European defence procurement.

## The demand signal is already unambiguous

Nine months before the debut, the organisers announced a fourth exhibition hall because the original three were at capacity. That took indoor space from 82,000 to 100,000 square metres, with a further 25,000 square metres outdoors. Half of the space had been booked or reserved early on, including by Rheinmetall, Hensoldt and Diehl Defence.

When exhibitors commit that hard to an event with no track record, it is because they expect the buyer side to show up.

## Why the structure matters for a component supplier

DSEI Germany is organised around the Bundeswehr’s own domain structure rather than a generic defence-show template: Land, Air and Space, Naval, Cyber and Information, Joint Support, International and Regional Pavilions — and a dedicated **Industrial Solutions** zone.

That last one is the interesting part if you are not an OEM. Industrial Solutions exists for manufacturing and production capability that could be redirected toward defence work. If you make precision components, do contract machining, supply materials or run production capacity that happens to be certified for something adjacent, that zone is written for you.

## What "first edition" actually gets you

At an established show, the buyers you want have walked the same halls for a decade and already know who they buy from. Breaking in means displacing someone.

At a first edition, three things are briefly true at once:

1. **No incumbent floor relationships.** Nobody has "their" supplier at this show yet.
2. **Buyers are actively mapping.** Procurement teams attend a debut edition specifically to see what exists.
3. **Attention is not yet rationed.** At a mature show, a senior buyer’s calendar is full before they arrive. At a first edition, it is not.

That window closes after the 2027 edition. By 2029 the floor will have a memory.

## What to do about it now

**Get accreditation moving early.** Defence shows vet attendees. Lead times are longer than you expect and the paperwork is specific.

**Settle your export-control position before you write a single line of marketing.** If your products touch controlled technology, what may be discussed and shown is a question for your compliance team, not your sales team, and certainly not for a representative. Decide this first.

**Target the Industrial Solutions zone and the regional pavilions**, not the primes' halls. The primes will be mobbed. The supply-chain conversations happen elsewhere.

**Book meetings in the autumn, not in February.** For a show this oversubscribed, the diaries fill long before the doors open.
