Most export managers budget a German trade fair as a flight and a hotel. The flight and the hotel are the small part.

Here is the honest list. Numbers are indicative ranges for a two-person trip from an Indian metro to a four-day show in Germany, booked at normal notice rather than six months out.

## The visible costs

**Flights.** Two return economy tickets to Frankfurt, Munich or Hannover, booked six to eight weeks ahead. More if the show is in a secondary city and you need a connection.

**Hotel.** This is where people get caught. Hotel rates in a fair city during fair week are not normal rates. Rooms near the venue are booked out by exhibitors months ahead, and what remains is priced accordingly. Budget two rooms for five nights, and expect the fair-week multiple.

**Fair entry.** A trade visitor day pass is modest. A multi-day pass for two people is not nothing, but it is the smallest line on this list.

**Ground transport, meals, local travel.** Five days of two people eating and moving around a German city.

## The costs nobody budgets

**Visa processing.** The German missions in India have publicly flagged longer appointment waiting times and longer processing times because of demand. There is a dedicated appointment category for urgent business travel and trade fair exhibitors — but the German mission is explicit that booking under that category does not speed up the decision itself, and processing can still take up to fifteen days, longer if documents are incomplete.

So the real cost is not the visa fee. It is that your show date is fixed and your approval date is not.

**Rejection risk.** Indian applicants face a meaningful refusal rate across Schengen countries. If a refusal lands three weeks before the show, you have paid for flights and a hotel you cannot use, and you have lost the show.

**Lost working time.** Two senior people, out of the business for a week once travel is counted. For an SME, that is the most expensive line on the page and it never appears in the budget.

**The one-show ceiling.** Having spent all of the above, you attend one fair. The European calendar is dense — electronica and Space Tech Expo Europe fall a week apart in November 2026, Formnext overlaps Space Tech almost exactly, and LIGNA 2027 has been deliberately scheduled just before interzum in Cologne so that a single trip can cover both. A team flying in from India realistically captures one of those. Someone already in Europe captures several.

## What the same budget buys instead

This is the arithmetic that started this company. The budget for one two-person trip to one show covers representation at several shows, across more than one country, with the meetings booked in advance in German.

That is not an argument for never coming to Europe. Meeting your buyers in person matters, and eventually you should. It is an argument for finding out whether the demand is real before you spend a week and a visa cycle discovering it.
