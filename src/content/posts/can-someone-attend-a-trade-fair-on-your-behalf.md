The short answer is yes, with one important limit that most people get wrong.

## What is fine

Attending a German trade fair as a trade visitor on behalf of another company is ordinary business. Agents, consultants, distributors and sourcing representatives do it at every show. Carrying a letter of authorisation and introducing yourself as a company’s European representative is normal and expected.

Governments do this as a paid service. The US Commercial Service sells a "Trade Show Representation" product to American exporters that involves pre-show promotion, representing the client at the overseas show, displaying their promotional materials and conducting outreach to buyers in attendance — priced by company size. So the model is not a loophole. It is an established trade-promotion instrument that happens not to be available to Indian exporters.

## What is not fine

Here is the part people miss. German fairgrounds prohibit handing out marketing material or soliciting business anywhere outside a rented stand.

Messe Berlin’s house rules state that distributing printed matter and advertising material on the grounds requires prior written permission. Messe Frankfurt’s rules state that without express permission, advertising of any kind, offering goods and services of any kind, and taking orders outside exhibition stands is not permitted. Leipziger Messe prohibits advertising and other commercial activity outside its own contractual arrangements. Some organisers go further and state explicitly that the rule applies to non-exhibitors conducting solicitation on the grounds, with charges applied if it is discovered afterwards.

Enforcement is real. The consequence is removal from the grounds, exclusion from the event, or a ban.

So the model of "walk the halls, hand out brochures, collect cards" works once and then stops working. If anyone offers you that service, they are selling you a risk they have not priced.

## The model that stays inside the rules

The difference between a floor-walker and a representative is where the conversation happens and whether it was arranged.

**Meetings are booked before the show.** Research the exhibitor list, identify the companies worth talking to, contact them by email and phone in the weeks beforehand, and agree a time. The conversation then happens at their stand, by their invitation, which is the one place a commercial conversation is unambiguously welcome.

**Use the organiser’s own matchmaking.** Many European shows run free structured B2B programmes. Enterprise Europe Network runs twenty-minute matchmaking meetings at Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen across all three days, with dedicated tables in two halls, and runs Technology & Business Cooperation Days on the Hannover Messe grounds. These are sanctioned, scheduled, free, and open to visitors rather than exhibitors only. They are the single most underused asset in European trade fairs.

**Materials travel to meetings, not to strangers.** A brochure handed to someone who agreed to meet you is not distribution of advertising material on the grounds. A brochure pushed at a passer-by in an aisle is.

**If you want to work the aisles, rent the space.** That is what stand space is for, and it is why stand staffing and turnkey stands exist as separate services.
